Our N4 lysis paper is out

How phage N4 opens its host cell, now published in the Journal of Bacteriology.

Written by Michael Baffour Awuah  ·  PhD Candidate, Ramsey Lab · Texas A&M University

I am really excited to share that our paper on phage N4 lysis is now published in the Journal of Bacteriology!

Phages end every infection by breaking their host cell open. N4 is unusual: under the right conditions it holds off on lysis for hours, a state called lysis inhibition, and releases far more phage per cell as a result. In this paper we defined the proteins N4 uses to lyse E. coli: a SAR endolysin–holin system with its spanins. We showed that this lysis cassette is enough to bring on lysis inhibition when phages are dense, and we mapped regions of the genome, inside and outside the cassette, that are involved in it.
